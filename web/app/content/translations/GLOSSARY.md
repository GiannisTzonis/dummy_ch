# Translation glossary (fr / it / de / es)

Every translation (interface wording and page text) uses these terms. Translate from the English;
when the English is unclear, the Greek page is the tie-breaker. Do not invent facts, soften or
embellish: same meaning, same facts, natural wording in the target language.

## Names: never translated

- People, choirs, ensembles, venues abroad, works, festivals and organisations abroad keep the
  spelling of the English page (Latin script): "Ambrož Čopi", "Vox Humana", "Voci Contra Tempo",
  "Gøtu Kirkju Ungdómskór", "Choral Festival Network (CFN)", "Ave Maria".
- Greek people and Greek choirs: the Latin spelling used on the English page ("Kosmas Koronaios",
  "Theodora Pavlovitch"), never re-transliterated.
- Titles of musical works stay as written (also when they are English).
- **Choir names stay exactly as listed in the English, descriptive words included**, everywhere
  (schedules, headings, biographies): "Female Chamber Choir Caloroso", "Children's Choir
  "Okarina"", "Choir PRIMO Secondary School nr1". They are the choirs' registered names; a
  translated form in one place and the listed form in another would read as two choirs. Only the
  festival's own choirs use the forms in the table below. In a heading or list line "Choir X,
  Bucharest, Romania", the name stays as listed and the town and country after it are written the
  target language's way ("Choir X, Bucarest, Roumanie"). Quotation marks inside a name take the
  target language's form (“Cantanti” Children’s Choir -> « Cantanti » Children’s Choir /
  „Cantanti“ Children’s Choir). This covers every choir named as taking
  part in an event the site reports (festival, competitions, concerts in the news); a choir only
  mentioned in passing in someone's biography may be described in the target language.
- Towns and countries, Greek or foreign: the usual name in the target language where one exists
  (Athènes / Atene / Athen / Atenas; Thessalonique / Salonicco / Thessaloniki / Tesalónica;
  Cracovie / Cracovia / Krakau / Cracovia; Lubiana for Ljubljana in Italian), otherwise the English
  spelling (Preveza, Parga, Iasi). Nikopolis: Nicopolis / Nicopoli / Nikopolis / Nicópolis.
- Organisations: an official name stays as it is (Westminster Choir College, ACDA, Choral Festival
  Network); where the English page itself only describes a foreign institution in English
  ("Academy of Music in Ljubljana"), it may be described in the target language. The same for
  Greek events the English only describes ("Tyrnavos Music Festival", "Thessaly Choir Festival"):
  described in the target language. Official names of networks, festivals and competitions abroad
  stay as they are (Choral Festival Network, Tolosa International Choral Competition).

## Form

- Times: always the 24-hour clock, written as in the published pages — French and Italian "20:30",
  German "20:30 Uhr" (or "20:30" in timetables), Spanish "20:30"; a range "04:00 - 08:00 p.m."
  becomes "16:00 - 20:00".
- Quotation marks: French « » (with a space inside: « Armonia »), Italian «», German „ “, Spanish
  «». Titles of works in a list keep the marks the English gives them.
- Centuries: the target language's usual form — Roman numerals in French, Italian and Spanish
  (XVIe siècle, XVI secolo, siglo XVI), digits in German (16. Jahrhundert).
- Italian ordinals agree with the noun: 44° Festival, 7ª edizione / masterclass.
- Spanish ordinals agree with the noun: 44.º Festival, 7.ª edición / clase magistral; 1.er and 3.er
  before a masculine noun (1.er premio).

- Dates, times, numbers and scores exactly as in the English, written the target language's way:
  "6–12 July 2026" -> "6–12 juillet 2026" / "6–12 luglio 2026" / "6.–12. Juli 2026" /
  "6–12 de julio de 2026"; times as in the English ("10:00", "8:30 p.m." -> "20h30" / "20:30" /
  "20:30 Uhr" / "20:30"). Prices "€20" -> "20 €" (fr, de, es), "20 €" (it).
- Ordinals: "44th" -> "44e" / "44°" / "44." / "44.º".
- Headings, labels and buttons: the target language's usual capitalisation (French and Italian:
  sentence case; German: nouns capitalised; Spanish: sentence case). A label printed in capitals in
  the English stays in capitals.
- Formal address to visitors: vous / Lei (or impersonal) / Sie / usted.
- Keep markdown exactly: `[text](link)` (translate the text, never the link), `**bold**`, line
  breaks. Keep every block: one translated block per English block, in the same order.
